You step back from the whole of Earth. One globe, spinning in front of you, and every layer you've studied in isolation is finally together. Satellites drift at their true altitude — some hugging the planet, others far out in geostationary orbit. Weather stations, crop regions, Mars and Moon analog sites, thousands of active fire cells and fresh quakes — all plotted, all toggleable. Time-lapse the orbits and watch the machine of the sky move. No more jumping between modules. One question, one answer: where is everything on Earth, right now. Positions are approximate; the fires and quakes are real.
