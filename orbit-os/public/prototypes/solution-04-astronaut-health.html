Astronaut Health Monitor (prototype)

Daily self-check and wearable-style indicators, compared with each astronaut's own early-mission baseline. Demo only, for a single simulated crew member.

NOT MEDICAL ADVICE. This prototype uses generated sample data and placeholder thresholds. It does not diagnose or treat anything. Any flag must be reviewed by the crew medical officer or flight surgeon.

Absolute placeholder limits (also must be set by the medical team)

  • Mood at or below 2 of 5 PLACEHOLDER

Baseline = mean and spread of the first 14 mission days. Spread has a minimum floor so small noise does not trigger alerts. A flag is shown when a value moves in the concerning direction. A flag marked "repeat" also fired the day before.

    Next steps to use real data

    • Replace the generated series with the crew's own onboard health logs, through the mission's medical data system and the flight surgeon's approval.
    • Set every threshold and sensitivity value with the NASA Human Research Program and the medical team. Current values are placeholders.
    • Use NASA Lifetime Surveillance of Astronaut Health (LSAH) and the Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA) to study which indicator ranges are typical for spaceflight.
    • Check any data-handling plan with the privacy, consent and security rules that apply to crew medical records before use.
    • Test alert timing and wording with flight surgeons and crew in a simulation before any real use.
